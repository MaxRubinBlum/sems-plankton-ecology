# Manuscript architecture freeze

## Central framing

Vertical stratification is the environmental premise, not the principal discovery. The manuscript focuses on objectively detected ecological transition zones, their trophic meaning, and coordinated prokaryotic–eukaryotic community turnover.

Working central claim:

> The Eastern Mediterranean water column is not characterized simply by gradual microbial replacement with depth. Independent prokaryotic, eukaryotic and trophic data identify a common ecological transition between the DCM and upper mesopelagic, while strong cross-domain concordance shows that microbial communities reorganize coherently across and within these vertically structured habitats.

Use "cross-domain community concordance" or "coordinated cross-domain turnover" for the measured result. Ecological coupling may be discussed as an interpretation, but direct interactions are not demonstrated.

## Main Results and figures

### Result 1. Hydrography defines the dominant vertical template of microbial community organization

- Strong vertical gradients in temperature, salinity, oxygen and fluorescence.
- Both 16S and 18S communities show pronounced vertical differentiation.
- Hydrographic PC1 independently explains approximately 29% of 16S and 17% of 18S compositional variation.
- Hydrography is substantially stronger than geography, season or year.

### Result 2. Independent microbial data layers converge on an upper-water ecological transition

Formal continuous breakpoint scan, controlling station × cruise profile:

- 16S best breakpoint: ~170 m; partial R² ~0.33; bootstrap 95% interval ~142–230 m.
- 18S best breakpoint: ~220 m; partial R² ~0.23; bootstrap interval concentrated ~210–220 m.
- 18S trophic/ecological traits: ~220 m; partial R² ~0.23; broader bootstrap interval ~120–220 m.
- Max-statistic permutation P = 0.002 for all three datasets.
- Independent estimates converge on a broader ecological transition zone of approximately 150–250 m.
- Interpret prokaryotic restructuring as beginning somewhat shallower than the strongest eukaryotic/trophic transition; do not claim a universal sharp boundary at exactly 220 m.

### Result 3. The transition separates contrasting taxonomic and trophic regimes

- Curated 16S and 18S taxonomic succession across a common vertical axis.
- Bona fide phototrophy and constitutive mixotrophy concentrated in illuminated waters and decline across the upper transition.
- Phototrophic signal tracks measured fluorescence independently of categorical depth assignment.
- Below the DCM, increased representation of Syndiniales-associated parasitism and radiolarian-associated phagotrophy.
- Deeper waters show increasing diplonemid heterotrophy.
- Use "trophic and ecological trait categories", not generic "guilds".
- Adjacent-depth turnover independently supports concentrated taxonomic and functional restructuring around the DCM/upper mesopelagic transition.

Secondary deep result in main text/inset:
- In samples >=600 m, objective near-bottom status explains ~16.3% of 18S variation after controlling station, bottom depth, year and season; P = 0.001.
- Near-bottom transition includes declines in Syndiniales Group I, Spumellaria and Acantharea and enrichment of several heterotrophic protist groups.
- Treat as a secondary, domain-asymmetric boundary rather than co-equal with the upper transition.

### Result 4. Prokaryotic and eukaryotic community turnover remains concordant beyond measured hydrographic filtering

Across 248 paired 16S/18S samples:
- Raw whole-community Bray–Curtis concordance: Spearman rho = 0.896, permutation P = 0.001.
- After residualizing hydrographic PC1, PC2 and fluorescence: rho = 0.740, P = 0.001.
- Significant concordance persists within every major ecological depth stratum.

Within-zone rho:
- Surface: 0.569
- Near-surface: 0.686
- DCM: 0.504
- Below DCM: 0.887
- 300–600 m: 0.433
- >600 m: 0.725
- Near-bottom: 0.690

The especially strong below-DCM concordance coincides with the independently detected transition/trophic reorganization.

Interpretation limit:
> Prokaryotic and eukaryotic community turnover is coordinated beyond that attributable to the measured hydrographic gradient alone.

Do not claim that residual concordance demonstrates direct biological interactions; unmeasured shared environmental structure remains possible.

### Result 5. Geography and season modify rather than override the vertical ecological template

- Coastal/shelf/offshore differentiation is secondary to vertical hydrographic organization.
- Geographic effects remain after hydrographic control: approximately 2.4% of 16S and 2.3% of 18S variation.
- Primary coastal/offshore comparisons should be restricted to surface/near-surface samples to avoid station bathymetry confounding.
- Seasonality is strongest in upper waters and weakens with depth.
- Functional-neighborhood geography belongs here only if it adds beyond whole-community patterns.

# Main figure legends

## Figure 1. Hydrographic structure and vertical organization of microbial communities along the Eastern Mediterranean transect.

**a,** Sampling design along the coastal-to-offshore H transect. Points indicate samples used for 18S rRNA gene analyses, positioned according to sampling depth and station; colors denote predefined vertical habitat categories and symbols distinguish winter and summer sampling. Horizontal marks indicate median bottom depth and vertical bars show the observed bottom-depth range among CTD casts at each station. Depth is shown on a linear scale. **b,** Principal component analysis of hydrographic conditions associated with microbial samples, based on standardized CTD temperature, salinity, dissolved oxygen and fluorescence. PC1 and PC2 explain 57.1% and 25.5% of hydrographic variation, respectively. Arrows indicate the direction and strength of correlations between the individual hydrographic variables and the two principal components. A complementary analysis incorporating nutrient and carbonate-system measurements is shown in Supplementary Fig. X. **c,d,** Principal coordinate analyses of prokaryotic (**c**, 16S rRNA gene) and microbial eukaryotic (**d**, 18S rRNA gene) community composition based on Bray–Curtis dissimilarities. Colors denote vertical habitat categories and circles and triangles denote winter and summer samples, respectively. Percentages on ordination axes indicate the proportion of variation represented by each axis.

## Figure 2. Microbial communities undergo sequential vertical reorganization modulated by season.

**a,** Pooled boundary scans across the upper water column for prokaryotic 16S community composition, microbial eukaryotic 18S community composition and 18S-derived trophic/ecological trait composition. For each candidate boundary depth, the plotted partial R² is the additional community variation associated with being above versus below that depth after accounting for station × cruise profile. Curves extend from the shallowest statistically supported threshold (10 m) to 420 m, and filled points mark the strongest supported breakpoint for each biological data layer. Transparent blue and orange vertical density fields show the distributions of winter and summer DCM fluorescence-peak depths, respectively, derived independently from CTD profiles; up to two ecologically meaningful fluorescence maxima were retained per cast when supported by peak prominence. **b,** Profile-bootstrap distributions of the strongest pooled breakpoint for each biological data layer. Thin lines show 95% bootstrap intervals, thick lines show interquartile ranges and points show medians. **c,** Boundary scans repeated separately for winter and summer. Biological data layers are colored as in **a**; winter is shown by solid lines/circles and summer by dashed lines/triangles. DCM density fields are shown as in **a**. **d,** Season-specific profile-bootstrap breakpoint distributions. Symbols distinguish winter and summer; interval and point summaries are defined as in **b**. Boundary significance in the pooled analysis was evaluated using a max-statistic permutation test that permuted depth assignments within station × cruise profiles while retaining the full threshold search; all three biological data layers were significant at P = 0.002. The seasonal analysis was used to assess displacement of the inferred transitions rather than to redefine the primary pooled test.

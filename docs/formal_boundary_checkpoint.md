# Formal ecological boundary analysis checkpoint

## Question

Can the principal upper-water microbial transition be detected directly from continuous biological data rather than imposed using predefined depth categories?

## Approach

Candidate boundary depths from 60 to 420 m were scanned independently for:

1. 16S whole-community composition;
2. 18S whole-community composition;
3. five 18S trophic/ecological trait categories.

For each candidate depth, separation attributable to being above versus below the threshold was evaluated after accounting for station × cruise profile. Station×cruise profiles were then cluster-bootstrapped to estimate breakpoint uncertainty. A max-statistic permutation procedure, shuffling depth assignments within profiles, tested the significance of the best breakpoint while accounting for the fact that multiple candidate thresholds were scanned.

## Results

### 16S

- Best threshold: ~170 m
- Maximum partial R²: ~0.33
- Profile-bootstrap 95% interval: approximately 142–230 m
- Max-statistic permutation P = 0.002

### 18S

- Best threshold: ~220 m
- Maximum partial R²: ~0.23
- Bootstrap distribution tightly concentrated around ~210–220 m
- Max-statistic permutation P = 0.002

### 18S trophic/ecological traits

- Best threshold: ~220 m
- Maximum partial R²: ~0.23
- Broader bootstrap interval, approximately 120–220 m
- Max-statistic permutation P = 0.002

The permutation null distributions produced maximal partial R² values only around ~0.004–0.008, far below the observed maxima.

## Interpretation

The three biological layers independently recover an overlapping upper-water ecological transition. The appropriate manuscript interpretation is a broader **~150–250 m transition zone**, not a universal sharp boundary at 220 m.

Prokaryotic restructuring appears to begin somewhat shallower (~170 m), whereas the strongest eukaryotic compositional and trophic reorganization occurs closer to ~220 m. Thus the domains traverse the same broader ecological transition but need not respond at exactly the same depth.

This transition lies below/around the lower margin of the DCM system and marks entry into the upper mesopelagic microbial ecosystem.

## Manuscript significance

This analysis changes the novelty claim. The manuscript does not merely show that predefined surface, DCM and mesopelagic communities differ. Instead, independent biological data themselves identify where community and trophic restructuring is concentrated.

Preferred claim:

> Continuous depth-resolved analysis identified a major microbial ecological transition between the DCM and upper mesopelagic. Independent breakpoint estimates from prokaryotic community composition, eukaryotic community composition and protistan trophic organization converged within a common ~150–250 m transition zone.

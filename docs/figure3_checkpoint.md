## Figure 3 legend

**Figure 3 | Seasonal restructuring of vertical taxonomic succession and trophic organization across the Eastern Mediterranean water column.** **a,b,** Vertical distributions of dominant and depth-responsive prokaryotic (**a**, 16S rRNA gene) and microbial eukaryotic (**b**, 18S rRNA gene) taxa during winter and summer. Taxa were selected from the pooled dataset, independently of season, based on their abundance and strength of vertical differentiation; identical taxa and ordering are therefore shown for both seasons. Taxa are ordered from shallow- to deep-associated groups according to their abundance-weighted vertical distributions. Heatmap values are standardized within each taxon across both seasons (row *z*-scores), allowing direct comparison of relative enrichment between winter and summer. Colored strips indicate the vertical habitat categories used throughout the study. Circles indicate the maximum median relative abundance attained by each taxon across depth layers and seasons, with circle size proportional to abundance as indicated by the size key. **c,** Continuous depth distributions of major trophic traits inferred from 18S taxonomic composition: bona fide phototrophy, constitutive mixotrophy, parasitism, radiolarian phagotrophy, and diplonemid heterotrophy. Solid and dashed lines show locally estimated median relative abundances in winter and summer, respectively; shaded regions indicate the corresponding interquartile ranges and faint points show individual samples. Horizontal dotted lines indicate the major ecological transition depths identified independently by the boundary analyses in Fig. 2 (~120, ~170 and ~220 m). Trait panels use independent horizontal scales to resolve their contrasting vertical responses.

## Supplementary legend

**Supplementary Figure X | Seasonal variation in fine-scale prokaryotic taxonomic succession across the Eastern Mediterranean water column.** Heatmaps show the vertical distributions of 30 abundant and depth-responsive 16S rRNA gene taxonomic groups during winter and summer. Taxa were selected from the pooled dataset, independently of season, based on their abundance and strength of vertical differentiation, and the same taxa and ordering are shown for both seasons. Taxa are ordered from shallow- to deep-associated groups according to their abundance-weighted vertical distributions. Because several lineages could not be confidently resolved to named genera, labels represent the genus or lowest taxonomic group resolved by the reference taxonomy. Heatmap values are standardized within each taxon across both seasons (row *z*-scores), allowing direct comparison of relative enrichment between winter and summer while emphasizing vertical distribution rather than absolute abundance. Colored strips indicate the vertical habitat categories used throughout the study. Circles indicate the maximum median relative abundance attained by each taxon across depth layers and seasons, with circle size proportional to abundance as indicated by the size key.

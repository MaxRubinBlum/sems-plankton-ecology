# Cross-domain 16S–18S concordance checkpoint

## Whole-community concordance

Using 248 samples with paired 16S and 18S data and successful CTD matching, pairwise Bray–Curtis dissimilarities are strongly concordant between prokaryotic and eukaryotic communities:

- Spearman rho = 0.896
- permutation P = 0.001

Thus, samples that are compositionally different in 16S tend also to be compositionally different in 18S.

## Concordance beyond hydrography

To test whether this relationship is simply caused by both domains responding to the vertical hydrographic gradient, PCoA coordinates were residualized against hydrographic PC1, hydrographic PC2 and CTD fluorescence. Distances in the residual ordination spaces remained strongly concordant:

- residual rho = 0.740
- permutation P = 0.001

This supports coordinated cross-domain community organization beyond the dominant shared hydrographic structure. This should be described as cross-domain ecological coupling/concordance, not evidence of direct biological interactions.

## Within-depth concordance

The relationship persists within individual depth strata:

| Depth zone | n | rho | permutation P |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Surface | 39 | 0.569 | 0.002 |
| Near-surface | 51 | 0.686 | 0.002 |
| DCM | 27 | 0.504 | 0.002 |
| Below DCM | 42 | 0.887 | 0.002 |
| 300–600 m | 43 | 0.433 | 0.002 |
| >600 m | 31 | 0.725 | 0.002 |
| Near-bottom | 15 | 0.690 | 0.002 |

The strongest within-zone concordance occurs below the DCM, coinciding with the region of strongest taxonomic and functional vertical turnover.

## Shallow coastal/offshore comparison

For matched surface + near-surface samples, cross-domain concordance is present on both sides of the coastal/offshore transition:

- coastal: rho = 0.638, P = 0.002
- offshore: rho = 0.720, P = 0.002

Thus, the whole-community relationship is not generated solely by mixing coastal and offshore habitats.

## Integration with current manuscript model

Current evidence supports the following hierarchy:

1. Hydrographic vertical structure is the dominant organizer of both 16S and 18S communities.
2. Objective ecological boundaries occur around the DCM/upper mesopelagic and, for protists especially, near the seabed.
3. Protistan trophic organization changes across these boundaries.
4. Prokaryotic and eukaryotic whole-community turnover is strongly coordinated.
5. Cross-domain concordance remains strong after removing the major hydrographic gradient and within individual depth zones.
6. Specific protistan trophic modes have distinct depth-controlled prokaryotic ecological neighborhoods.

These results motivate a central framing around vertical cross-domain ecological organization rather than parallel descriptive 16S and 18S biogeography.

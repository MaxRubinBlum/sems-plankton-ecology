# Main figure legends

## Figure 1. Hydrography provides the dominant vertical template for microbial community organization.

**a,** Distribution of 18S sampling depths across the principal coastal-to-offshore stations (H15, H25 and H01–H06). Horizontal marks indicate the median bottom depth recorded for each station and vertical bars show the observed range among CTD casts. Samples are colored by ecological depth layer (surface, near-surface, deep chlorophyll maximum [DCM], below DCM, 300–600 m, >600 m and near-bottom) and symbols distinguish winter (circles) from summer (triangles). Depth is shown on a linear scale. **b,** Hydrographic principal-component analysis based on CTD temperature, salinity, dissolved oxygen and fluorescence for samples with successful CTD interpolation. Samples are colored and shaped as in **a**. **c,d,** Principal-coordinate analyses of Bray–Curtis dissimilarity for prokaryotic 16S (**c**) and eukaryotic 18S (**d**) communities. Colors denote ecological depth layer and symbols denote season as in **a**. Percentages on axes indicate the fraction of positive-eigenvalue variation represented by each PCoA axis.

## Figure 2. Microbial communities undergo sequential vertical reorganization modulated by season.

**a,** Pooled boundary scans across the upper water column for prokaryotic 16S community composition, eukaryotic 18S community composition and 18S-derived trophic/ecological trait composition. For each candidate boundary depth, the plotted partial R² is the additional community variation associated with being above versus below that depth after accounting for station × cruise profile. Curves extend from the shallowest statistically supported threshold (10 m) to 420 m; filled points mark the strongest supported breakpoint for each biological data layer. Transparent blue and orange vertical density fields show the distributions of winter and summer DCM fluorescence-peak depths, respectively, derived directly from CTD profiles; up to two ecologically meaningful fluorescence maxima were retained per cast when supported by peak prominence. **b,** Profile-bootstrap distributions of the strongest pooled breakpoint for each biological data layer. Thin lines show 95% bootstrap intervals, thick lines show interquartile ranges and points show medians. **c,** Boundary scans repeated separately for winter and summer. Biological data layers are colored as in **a**; winter is shown by solid lines/circles and summer by dashed lines/triangles. DCM density fields are shown as in **a**. **d,** Season-specific profile-bootstrap breakpoint distributions. Symbols distinguish winter and summer; interval and point summaries are defined as in **b**. Boundary significance in the pooled analysis was evaluated using a max-statistic permutation test that permuted depth assignments within station × cruise profiles while retaining the full threshold search; all three biological data layers were significant at P = 0.002. The seasonal analysis was used to assess displacement of the inferred transitions rather than to redefine the primary pooled test.

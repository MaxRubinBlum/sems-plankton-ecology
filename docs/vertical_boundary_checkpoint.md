# Vertical ecological-boundary checkpoint

## Objective adjacent-depth turnover

Community turnover was quantified between adjacent sampled depths within each cruise × station profile. Taxonomic turnover used Bray–Curtis dissimilarity; 18S functional turnover used Euclidean distance across standardized conservative trait axes. Turnover was inspected both as raw distance and normalized by the log10 depth interval.

The main reproducible upper-water transition spans approximately 80–400 m. For 16S, the strongest named transitions are DCM → below DCM and below DCM → 300–600 m. For 18S, high turnover extends from near-surface → DCM through DCM → below DCM and below DCM → 300–600 m. Functional 18S turnover independently identifies the same region.

This transition coincides with declining fluorescence, cooling, and lower oxygen below the DCM, supporting interpretation as a hydrographically structured ecological boundary rather than an arbitrary depth-bin effect.

## Deep near-bottom boundary robustness

A second apparent 18S transition was tested independently using samples ≥600 m. Near-bottom status was defined objectively as either ≤100 m above the seabed or sample depth ≥85% of total water-column depth.

After controlling for station, bottom depth, year, and season, near-bottom status explained 16.3% of deep 18S community variation (station-stratified permutation P = 0.001; n = 47 deep samples, 17 near-bottom). Within station/cruise profiles, nearest deep-water versus near-bottom pairs had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity ≈0.50.

Among conservative 18S functional traits, parasitism decreased significantly toward the seabed after the same controls (FDR ≈0.031). Diplonemid heterotrophy tended to increase near-bottom but did not survive FDR correction (nominal P ≈0.045; FDR ≈0.113). Radiolarian phagotrophy did not show a significant independent near-bottom shift.

## Interpretation

Current evidence supports two vertical ecological transitions:

1. An upper transition from the DCM through the upper mesopelagic, shared by 16S taxonomy, 18S taxonomy, and 18S trophic organization.
2. A deep near-bottom transition that is particularly strong for microbial eukaryotes and is not explained by station or bottom-depth differences.

The deep boundary should next be decomposed taxonomically to determine whether it is driven by coherent ecological groups rather than rare ASVs.
